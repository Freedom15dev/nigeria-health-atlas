1/ Halima is 52 and lives in Bauchi. Her kidneys have failed: dialysis 2 to 3 times a week, for life. (An illustration, not a real person.) I built a public-data atlas to see what she'd face. 🧵

2/ Distance: half of Bauchi's people live more than 72 km (straight line) from a dialysis unit known to work. In 7 states, at least 8 in 10 people live more than 100 km away.

3/ Cost: about ₦45,000 a session at a public hospital, paid by the family. ₦4.7m a year at two sessions a week. Only 3.9% of adults aged 15 to 49 have any health insurance (NDHS 2024).

4/ The counts don't agree. Dialysis centres: 80 to 200+ depending on the source. Working radiotherapy machines: 4 to 'fewer than 20', against 120 needed.

5/ Why units fail, when sources say: mostly systems (power, water, consumables, maintenance), not just missing machines.

6/ One illustrative dialysis centre: 45 patients, but it receives ₦41,700 a session and needs ₦61,452 to break even. A weaker naira makes it worse: supplies are imported.

7/ Public data tells you where to look first. It can't replace checking each unit on the ground. Every number sourced and dated: https://nigeria-health-atlas.streamlit.app/
